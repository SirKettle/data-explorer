`@data-xplorer/core` draws tree- and graph-shaped data as a space you zoom through. This page
goes from install to a working explorer. [Concepts](/data-xplorer/concepts/) explains the ideas
behind it.

## Install

```sh
npm install @data-xplorer/core pixi.js react react-dom
```

`react` (18 or 19) and `pixi.js` (8.13.2 or later 8.x) are peer dependencies: your app provides
them. PixiJS is loaded lazily, the first time an explorer mounts, so it stays out of your page's
first load. The package is ESM only.

## Your first explorer

Everything below is one file. It describes a folder tree as a **scene source**: given a focus (a
folder path, or `null` for the top), it returns what to draw there. Zoom into a folder to enter it;
zoom out to leave.

</Demo>

Three things in it are worth knowing now:

- **Clusters and nodes.** Folders are `clusters`, drawn as soft clouds you can enter; files are
  `nodes`, drawn as discs. `weight` sets each one's size against its neighbours.
- **`drillsInto` and `parentOf`.** An item's `drillsInto` is the focus it opens. `parentOf` says
  where zooming out of a focus leads: here, back to the folder's own cloud in its parent.
- **`focus` and `onSettle`.** The explorer moves the camera itself as the viewer zooms.
  `onSettle(focus)` tells you where they came to rest, once per burst of motion. Store it (in state,
  or in your URL) and pass it back as `focus`; passing a different `focus` (Back, a deep link) makes
  the explorer jump there.

The explorer needs no stylesheet. Its canvas fills its container's width; if nothing gives it a
height, it takes 480 px. Give its container, or `.data-explorer__surface`, a height to change that.
