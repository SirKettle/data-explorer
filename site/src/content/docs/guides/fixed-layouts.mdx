Some data has places of its own: countries on a map, rooms on a floor plan, panels on a board. A
**fixed** scene draws each item at the rectangle you give it, at the size you give it, and a
**region** fills that rectangle, or the outline you give it, edge to edge. Zoom into an island
below: its districts appear exactly where it was drawn, and the other island stays where it was.
Point at the lake on South Isle: it is not part of the island.

- **`layout: "fixed"`** on a scene, and a **`box`** (`{ x, y, width, height }`) on each item, in
  units of your choosing. An item without a box is not drawn. `weight` is not used for size.
- **`bounds`** is the scene's extent in the same units; without it, the smallest rectangle round its
  items. A fixed scene **streamed in pages** should set it, or the extent grows with each page and
  everything already drawn moves.
- **Children land on their parent** when a fixed scene is entered from an item of a fixed scene:
  the child scene's `bounds` is fitted exactly onto the item's box. So keep one set of units all the
  way down, and give each scene its parent item's box as `bounds`.
- **`shape: "region"`** fills its box. With an **`outline`**, it is that shape instead: a list of
  polygons, each a list of rings (its edge, then its holes), each a list of `[x, y]` points, in the
  same units as the box. That is a GeoJSON MultiPolygon's `coordinates` once projected, so a
  `Polygon`'s coordinates go in as `[coordinates]`. Keep the outline inside the box.
- **Hit-testing** follows the outline: a hole is not part of the region, and nor is the box around
  it. Hover, selection and the `ring` emphasis are drawn along the outline.
- **Labels** go inside a region, in the middle of its box, or at **`labelAt`** (a point in the same
  units) when the middle of the box falls outside the shape.
- **The other shapes** work in a fixed scene too: a `square` fills its box, and a `disc` or `cloud`
  is the largest circle that fits it. A custom renderer gets the box as `frame.width` and
  `frame.height`.
- **Your units do not matter.** Each fixed scene is scaled so its longer side is 1,000 units of its
  own world, the size of any other scene's, so a small country deep in a map zooms in like anything
  else.
- **The accessible outline** lists a fixed scene's items in the order you give them, clusters first:
  for a map, alphabetical is usually right.
