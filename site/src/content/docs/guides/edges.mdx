A scene's `edges` always shape a radial scene's [layout](/data-explorer/concepts/#packed-radial-and-fixed-scenes).
With `edges={{ visible: true }}` they are also **drawn**, in packed and radial scenes alike: under
the items, fading with them through every transition, and culled off screen. An edge naming an item
that is not in the scene is skipped.

Each edge's style starts from `style` and is overridden by `styles[kind]`. In the demo, a module
sits in the middle: solid arrows to what it imports, dashed curves from what imports it.
Double-click a neighbour to walk the graph.

| Style | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `color` | `palette.label` | Any colour. |
| `width` | `1.5` | Screen pixels, whatever the zoom. |
| `alpha` | `0.5` | Multiplied by the lower opacity of the edge's two items. |
| `dash` | `null` | `[dash, gap]` in screen pixels, or `null` for solid. |
| `arrow` | `"none"` | `"start"`, `"end"` (at `targetId`) or `"both"`. |
| `curve` | `0` | Bows the edge sideways by this fraction of its length (−1 to 1). Edges both ways between one pair, with the same curve, part. |

**Every drawn edge is also in the outline**, as a link to its far end, so a relationship that can
be seen can also be followed from the keyboard and by a screen reader.

**Cap.** A scene draws at most `edges.max` edges (default 500). Over it, edges to the scene's
`focusId` come first, then the rest in order, and the outline says how many are shown. Dashes are
the expensive style: see [Performance](/data-explorer/guides/performance/).
